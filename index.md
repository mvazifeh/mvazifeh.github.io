## About Me

I'm a Senior Manager of ML Engineering at **Capital One**, where I lead ML and data engineering teams building multi-agent GenAI systems. Most of my work is about turning experimental agent workflows into systems people can rely on in production.

My training is in theoretical physics (PhD, University of British Columbia), and while finishing it I developed quantum optimization algorithms at 1QBit. The habit I took from physics has shaped everything since: model the system from first principles, then hold it accountable to data.

After my PhD I spent three years as a research lead at **<a href="https://senseable.mit.edu">MIT's Senseable City Lab</a>**, studying cities as complex systems through large-scale data. That work led to two *Nature* papers: a first-author paper solving the minimum fleet problem for on-demand mobility, and one establishing a universal visitation law of human mobility. It also included a collaboration with the Broad Institute and National Geographic on US population genomics, and three projects exhibited at the MIT Museum.

I then worked on anomaly detection at **Amazon Alexa AI**, and co-founded **<a href="https://www.adaviv.com">Adaviv</a>**, a VC-backed MIT spinoff, where as CTO and Head of AI I built a 27-person team across hardware, software, and AI to deploy computer vision for crop monitoring across farms in the US and Mexico.

I'm most interested in the **reliability of frontier AI systems**: where LLMs and agents break, how to measure it rigorously, and which architectural changes make them more reliable. With collaborators I built <a href="https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1714/">seqBench</a> (EMNLP 2025), a tunable benchmark that quantifies the limits of sequential reasoning in LLMs. I also mentored a student team at the University of Maryland on adversarial stress tests of RAG and context-retrieval patterns.
